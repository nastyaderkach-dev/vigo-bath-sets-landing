How an order gets its channel: rules and example
  1. Match GA4 transaction_id to the Shopify order number. Exclude B2B orders 2523, 2618, 2809, 2817, 3048, 3180 and 3186. Exclude 34 orders with zero original value from D2C purchase counts.
  2. Paid tables: Google uses “google / cpc” or a Shopify Google paid tag; Microsoft uses “bing / cpc” or a Microsoft tag; Meta uses facebook/instagram with cpc/paid or a Meta paid tag. Current check order: Google, Microsoft, Meta. Conflicting tags require order-level review, not a causal conclusion.
  3. Other orders retain the Shopify channel. Search includes organic and untagged ads. A product-feed tag alone does not prove a paid click. Do not allocate Direct, Unknown or Shop App proportionally to paid channels.
  4. Brand Search is identified by “brand” in the campaign name. Remaining Google orders enter PMax + Shopping. This residual grouping does not prove that PMax excludes branded demand.
  5. Example: an ad click is followed by a direct return visit and purchase. Shopify LND may retain the ad source; reconciliation depends on the source recorded for the purchase. For Oct 1–Nov 11 Google has 35 reconciled orders versus 45 LND orders. Different models do not automatically mean ten missing purchases.
Formulas, returns and reporting periods
  • Reconciled ROAS = sum of attributed orders’ value_net ÷ channel spend. Orders enter periods by order date. value_net is the exported value after discounts and recorded returns; completeness and consistent tax treatment remain unverified. This is not final ROAS after all returns.
  • CPA = spend ÷ attributed orders; Paid CAC = spend ÷ attributed new customers. A zero denominator means unavailable, not zero cost.
  • Three-channel MER = D2C Net Sales ÷ (Google + Meta + Microsoft spend). Excludes TikTok, possible Shop Campaigns spend, fees and other marketing costs. Dashboard Meta spend is account-wide; Netpeak separates performance and boosts.
  • Modeled ROMI = (attributed value × 30% − media spend) ÷ media spend. The 30% margin is unconfirmed. A 3.33× break-even applies only if 30% is contribution after the relevant variable costs and before advertising. This is neither accounting profit nor measured incremental lift.
  • Store Net Sales books returns by processing date. Cohort ROAS should deduct returns belonging to that order cohort as of a specified cutoff. These totals need not match. Exclude tax and shipping consistently; never deduct returns twice.
  • Store sales cover Aug 1–Nov 18, 2026. Reconciled channel ROAS covers Sep 2–Nov 18. GA4 was unavailable in August, so this reconciliation is unavailable; that does not imply native Shopify attribution cannot be obtained for August.
  • Nov 1–11 and Nov 12–18 are unequal intervals, not two full weeks. Compare daily pace and calculate rates from totals. Recent periods remain provisional because conversions and returns can arrive later.
Why sources differ and what remains open
  • Models, click/view windows, reporting dates, time zones, consent, missing tags, campaign scope, taxes and returns can differ. A discrepancy alone proves neither tracking failure nor zero channel contribution. Aim for explained differences rather than forced equality.
  • All 492 GA4 purchases matched Shopify: this describes matching of recorded GA4 purchases, not 100% coverage of Shopify orders. The Oct 1–Nov 11 audit found 256 of 278 Online Store orders (92.1%); the subset with a Shopify session was 253 of 260 (97.3%). Neither denominator equals all D2C orders.
  • Open issues: nine orders have differing value_net across versions; tax treatment in refunds; 100%-discount order exclusions in categories were aligned with the headline KPI on Nov 19 — category gross and net sales now reconciles. Category shares and returns / gross sales remain provisional.
  • October Google: 3,066 clicks / 41 conversions / $24,592.69 — verified directly against the Google Ads account on Nov 19. The separate Netpeak table (3,987 / 38 / $21,783.03) does not match the account and needs to be re-exported; purchase attribution does not explain click differences.
  • Proposed weekly standard: one D2C scope, reporting period and time zone; performance and boost spend separated; Shopify LND before returns and after cohort returns in separate columns; platform click/view results separately. Record export date, attribution window, reporting-date basis and validation status for every source. Proposed owners: analytics, PPC and VIGO; confirm assignments.

Shopify puts the feed tag on both free product listings and PMax clicks; in September — November 18, 14 of 28 such orders turned out to be PMax clicks and were moved to Google Ads. Why Direct is higher in August. Google tags don't reach the site, so some PMax clicks — from YouTube, Gmail and apps, where the browser doesn't pass the referrer — are recorded by Shopify as direct visits. Visits show it: in August PMax delivered 152 clicks a day; in September it was almost stopped (13 clicks a day), and Direct visits fell by 110 a day. In October PMax was restarted (+60 clicks a day) — and Direct visits rose by 59 a day. Since September GA4 helps separate such orders: 15 orders that Shopify recorded as Direct were moved to ad channels. There is no GA4 for August, so judging by PMax volume, August Direct includes roughly 10–15 more ad orders. Without them direct demand is flat — about 3 orders a day across the period, and per day Direct in November ($1.5K) is at the August level. October is weaker ($1.1K a day) because of returning customers: 11 versus 26 in September.

ROAS and ROMI. ROAS = order value ÷ spend: revenue per $1 of ads — revenue, not profit. ROMI = (order value × margin − spend) ÷ spend: how much ads earned or lost after paying for themselves. At a 30% margin ads break even at a 3.33× ROAS (ROMI = 0%): above it ROMI is positive, below it negative. Example: Google for Nov 12–18 — $5,526 of orders on $1,350 spend, 4.09× ROAS; $5,526 × 30% − $1,350 = +$308, +23% ROMI.

ROMI = (Shopify + GA4 reconciliation order value × 30% − spend) ÷ spend; the 30% margin is a working assumption from the brief. Account ROMI — the same using the value the platform claimed. Break-even at a 30% margin is a 3.33× ROAS. Brand Search is people already searching for VIGO, so its ROMI is not incremental lift. The all-period window for ads is September 2 — November 18: GA4 wasn’t running in August.

Takeaway
  • All period (Sep 2 — Nov 18): the reconciliation attributes $37.4K on $16.5K spend — 2.27× ROAS, −32% ROMI at a 30% margin. Only Google Brand Search is positive (+14% ROMI), but these are people already searching for VIGO. PMax and Shopping — −23%, Microsoft — −24%, Meta — −90%.
  • Nov 12–18 vs Nov 1–11: ROMI across channels rose from −35% to −7%: Google’s modeled result became positive (+23%: brand +104%, PMax and Shopping −13%), no orders were attributed to Meta or Microsoft in this reconciliation.
  • Meta from December: as agreed with Netpeak, after Black Friday Meta is not judged directly on ROAS or ROMI: most of its budget moves to boosts (reach, engagement) and background retargeting. Meta’s −90% is the fact for September 2 — November 18, not a December target; return expectations sit with Google and Bing.
  • What to do: Agree the margin and attribution basis before budget decisions. Modeled ROMI is provisional and does not establish incrementality.
