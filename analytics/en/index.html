More: what makes up the contribution, ROAS thresholds, conclusions and next steps
Key points
  • The business overall: before ads VIGO keeps 25% of net sales — $86K of $344K (up to ~30% if the shipping rates in VIGO’s file are per shipment rather than per unit). After all marketing ($29K) — $57K, or 17%.
  • Ads do not pay back on visible purchases. Break-even is a 4.0× ROAS in this dashboard and ≈4.6× in the accounts; for Google ≈4.1×, because its orders are mostly doors at a 25% margin. Actual ad ROAS is 2.27× (Sep 2 — Nov 22). Google Brand Search is closest to break-even (3.6×, −9% ROMI); PMax and Shopping −36%, Microsoft −40%, Meta −87%. PMax tROAS targets of 300–400% sit below break-even.
  • A paid customer costs more than they bring. A customer from ads costs $251 under the reconciliation, while their first order earns ≈$135. Repeat orders are 8%, so the first order has to pay back acquisition. In September ad spend was a third of August’s, yet new customers fell only 12%: ads add few customers beyond those who would come anyway. Part of this is seasonality — confirm with a test.
  • Shipping, not advertising, eats most of the margin: with pick & pack it is 34% of sales (ads — 9%), while customers paid only $4.0K for shipping. Freight for glass is $300 a unit, 35% of its price: doors earn 18–22%, screens 5%, and 52% of shower-enclosure sales were cancelled or returned.
  • Orders under $100 earn $4.4 on average, and they are almost one in five first orders from new customers (19%). The most profitable products are shower panels (40%) and faucets (30–36%), but ads barely push them; accessories (13%), liquidation (16%) and screens (5%) carry thin margins, and some items sell at a loss.
  • Possible missing costs: partner and ambassador codes drove $51.6K of gross sales. If they carry commissions, real marketing costs are higher.
  • What to do: 1) raise the ROAS threshold to 4.0× (Google ≈4.1×) and PMax tROAS to at least 460–500%; 2) shift budget to panels and faucets, and remove accessories, screens and liquidation from Shopping/PMax or give them low bids; 3) for glass — a freight surcharge or a price floor, no stacked discounts, investigate enclosure returns; 4) a free-shipping threshold (e.g. from $150–200) and bundles instead of cheap stand-alone accessories; 5) measure the real contribution of ads — Shopify LND and a holdout test in January; 6) get VIGO’s answers to the questions below — shipping decides whether the margin is 25% or 30%.

Methodology

Ad-order margins use the channel from the Shopify + GA4 reconciliation; the sample is small (73 orders). November cancellations are still coming in: 1–5% in November vs 6–10% in August to October.

ROAS and ROMI. ROAS = order value ÷ spend: revenue per $1 of ads — revenue, not profit. ROMI = (order value × margin − spend) ÷ spend: how much ads earned or lost after paying for themselves. At a 25% margin ads break even at a 4.0× ROAS (ROMI = 0%): above it ROMI is positive, below it negative. Example: Google for Nov 16–22 — $4,722 of orders on $1,568 spend, 3.01× ROAS; $4,722 × 25% − $1,568 = −$388, −25% ROMI.

ROMI = (Shopify + GA4 reconciliation order value × 25% − spend) ÷ spend; the 25% margin is actual — see “Margin and unit economics”. Account ROMI — the same using the value the platform claimed. Break-even at a 25% margin is a 4.0× ROAS. Brand Search is people already searching for VIGO, so its ROMI is not incremental lift. The all-period window for ads is September 2 — November 22: GA4 wasn’t running in August.

How an order gets its channel: rules and example
  1. Match GA4 transaction_id to the Shopify order number. Exclude B2B orders 2523, 2618, 2809, 2817, 3048, 3180 and 3186. Exclude 34 orders with zero original value from D2C purchase counts.
  2. Paid tables: Google uses “google / cpc” or a Shopify Google paid tag; Microsoft uses “bing / cpc” or a Microsoft tag; Meta uses facebook/instagram with cpc/paid or a Meta paid tag. Current check order: Google, Microsoft, Meta. Conflicting tags require order-level review, not a causal conclusion.
  3. Other orders retain the Shopify channel. Search includes organic and untagged ads. A product-feed tag alone does not prove a paid click. Do not allocate Direct, Unknown or Shop App proportionally to paid channels.
  4. Brand Search is identified by “brand” in the campaign name. Remaining Google orders enter PMax + Shopping. This residual grouping does not prove that PMax excludes branded demand.
  5. Example: an ad click is followed by a direct return visit and purchase. Shopify LND may retain the ad source; reconciliation depends on the source recorded for the purchase. For Oct 1–Nov 11 Google has 35 reconciled orders versus 45 LND orders. Different models do not automatically mean ten missing purchases.
Formulas, returns and reporting periods
  • Reconciled ROAS = sum of attributed orders’ value_net ÷ channel spend. Orders enter periods by order date. value_net is the exported value after discounts and recorded returns; completeness and consistent tax treatment remain unverified. This is not final ROAS after all returns.
  • CPA = spend ÷ attributed orders; Paid CAC = spend ÷ attributed new customers. A zero denominator means unavailable, not zero cost.
  • Three-channel MER = D2C Net Sales ÷ (Google + Meta + Microsoft spend). Excludes TikTok, possible Shop Campaigns spend, fees and other marketing costs. Dashboard Meta spend is account-wide; Netpeak separates performance and boosts.
  • Modeled ROMI = (attributed value × 25% − media spend) ÷ media spend. 25% is the actual contribution before advertising: Shopify sales minus product cost, shipping and pick & pack from VIGO’s cost file and payment fees (estimated at 2.5%), Aug 1 — Nov 22. Break-even is a 4.0× ROAS. This is neither accounting profit nor measured incremental lift.
  • Store Net Sales books returns by processing date. Cohort ROAS should deduct returns belonging to that order cohort as of a specified cutoff. These totals need not match. Exclude tax and shipping consistently; never deduct returns twice.
  • Store sales cover Aug 1–Nov 22, 2026. Reconciled channel ROAS covers Sep 2–Nov 22. GA4 was unavailable in August, so this reconciliation is unavailable; that does not imply native Shopify attribution cannot be obtained for August.
  • November uses Monday–Sunday weeks, as in Netpeak’s reports: Nov 2–8, 9–15 and 16–22; November 1 (a Sunday) belongs to October. Compare daily pace and calculate rates from totals. Recent periods remain provisional because conversions and returns can arrive later.
Why sources differ and what remains open
  • Models, click/view windows, reporting dates, time zones, consent, missing tags, campaign scope, taxes and returns can differ. A discrepancy alone proves neither tracking failure nor zero channel contribution. Aim for explained differences rather than forced equality.
  • All 522 GA4 purchases matched Shopify: this describes matching of recorded GA4 purchases, not 100% coverage of Shopify orders. The Oct 1–Nov 11 audit found 256 of 278 Online Store orders (92.1%); the subset with a Shopify session was 253 of 260 (97.3%). Neither denominator equals all D2C orders.
  • Open issues: nine orders have differing value_net across versions; tax treatment in refunds; 100%-discount order exclusions in categories were aligned with the headline KPI on Nov 19 — category gross and net sales now reconciles. Category shares and returns / gross sales remain provisional.
  • October Google: 3,066 clicks / 41 conversions / $24,592.69 — verified directly against the Google Ads account on Nov 19. The separate Netpeak table (3,987 / 38 / $21,783.03) does not match the account and needs to be re-exported; purchase attribution does not explain click differences.
  • Proposed weekly standard: one D2C scope, reporting period and time zone; performance and boost spend separated; Shopify LND before returns and after cohort returns in separate columns; platform click/view results separately. Record export date, attribution window, reporting-date basis and validation status for every source. Proposed owners: analytics, PPC and VIGO; confirm assignments.
