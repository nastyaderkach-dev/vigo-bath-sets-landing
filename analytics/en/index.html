* Why August has no Google Ads, what the feed tag is, and why Direct is higher in August

* In August GA4 was not yet collecting data, and Google UTM tags sit in the tracking template and barely reach the site, so August orders from Google ads ended up in Organic Search and Google Shopping (feed tag). Shopify puts the feed tag on both free product listings and PMax clicks; in September — November 14 of 25 such orders turned out to be PMax clicks and were moved to Google Ads. PMax spent $9.4K in August, so most of the 25 August orders worth $12.3K are probably ads too. Values are summed by order after returns, so they differ from D2C Net Sales below ($305.3K), which subtracts all returns processed in the period, including $16.8K on orders placed before August 1.

Why Direct is higher in August. Google tags don't reach the site, so some PMax clicks — from YouTube, Gmail and apps, where the browser doesn't pass the referrer — are recorded by Shopify as direct visits. Visits show it: in August PMax delivered 152 clicks a day; in September it was almost stopped (13 clicks a day), and Direct visits fell by 110 a day. In October PMax was restarted (+60 clicks a day) — and Direct visits rose by 59 a day. Since September GA4 helps separate such orders: 15 orders that Shopify recorded as Direct were moved to ad channels. There is no GA4 for August, so judging by PMax volume, August Direct includes roughly 10–15 more ad orders. Without them direct demand is flat — about 3 orders a day across the period, and per day Direct in November ($1.5K) is at the August level. October is weaker ($1.1K a day) because of returning customers: 11 versus 26 in September.

Customer acquisition cost — ad spend per new customer (first order in the store). Next to it — the gross profit that first order brings at a 30% margin: if acquisition cost is higher, the ads lose money on the first order. The 30% margin is a working assumption from the brief that needs confirmation; profit figures are estimates, not facts.

Methodology

A new customer is an order that is this buyer's first in the store's entire Shopify history; $0 orders are excluded. Channel — as in “Where purchases came from”: GA4 and Shopify tags. By channel we count from September 2, because GA4 wasn't running in August; the channel total is in the “Sep 2 — Nov 11” row. Spend — Meta and Google; Microsoft is not included because it isn't visible. Spend excludes agency fees and discounts: discounts are already deducted from order value.

Takeaway. For November 1–11 site conversion is 0.63% — the highest among the available periods (0.54–0.58% in other months); because the month is partial, the result is preliminary. Two steps drive the growth: 2.4% of visits add to cart (1.9% in September), and 40% complete a started checkout (36% in August).

Conclusions and recommendations

static data snapshot as of November 12 · plan updated November 14 · before BFCM on November 27

Key points

  1. D2C sales. For August 1 — November 11 Shopify Net Sales were $305.3K on 787 orders. This is the main measure of actual D2C sales.
  2. Funnel. Of 114,867 visits, 649 ended in a purchase — site conversion 0.57%. 787 orders were created in Shopify, of which 755 had a non-zero value before returns.
  3. Attribution. By Shopify last-click data, Direct and Organic Search account for 62.1% of order value. This figure includes some untagged paid traffic, so it does not reflect the pure organic contribution.
  4. Paid ads. For September 2 — November 11 Meta and Google brought $24.7K of attributed revenue on $11.9K spend, ROAS 2.07×. Under the working assumption of a 30% margin, the result after media spend is estimated at −$4.5K.
  5. Google Ads. Google brought 43 orders worth $23.4K on $8.49K spend, ROAS 2.76×. The channel brought 41 new customers at $207 each. At a 30% margin the first order brings about $159 of gross profit, so the channel doesn't yet pay back on the first purchase.
  6. Meta Ads. Meta brought 3 orders worth $1.23K on $3.41K spend, ROAS 0.36×. Until a confirmed result appears, the channel should be used only for limited tests.
  7. Microsoft Ads. For September 2 — November 11 Microsoft brought 16 orders worth $7.15K. Without spend data, CPA, ROAS and channel profitability can't be assessed.
  8. Categories. Shower doors make up 41.2% of gross sales and remain the main category for capturing demand. For shower enclosures, returns in the period equal 52.3% of gross sales, but this is not a cohort return rate.
  9. Mobile. On mobile 1.7% of visits add to cart versus 2.6% on desktop. The main CRO potential is before the cart step, but the specific cause needs to be found by analyzing product pages, landing pages and session recordings.
  10. Repeat purchases. Over 103 days returning customers placed 68 orders — 8.6% of the total. This period is too short to assess LTV; a cohort analysis over 90, 180 and 365 days is needed.
  11. Promo codes. A promo code was used in 38.5% of orders, and discounts total 4.7% of gross sales. To assess public and partner codes, measure not only sales but also the discount, commission and incrementality.
  12. Key next steps. Confirm margin and cost of goods, fix UTM attribution, get Microsoft Ads spend, optimize the mobile path to the cart, build a cohort returns report and assess customer LTV.

Methodology and differences between sources
  • Shopify is the main source for sales: net sales = gross − discounts − returns, excluding taxes and shipping.
  • Cross-channel analytics: Shopify orders are matched with GA4 purchases by order number — GA4 records the Shopify order ID as the transaction ID; all 434 GA4 purchases were found in Shopify. The Shopify channel is the last visit before purchase; actual ad results are orders that GA4 or a Shopify UTM tag attributed to a paid click. GA4 hasn't processed sources for November 11 orders yet, so the Shopify channel is used for them; November ad figures are a lower bound.
  • Meta Ads — VIGO account, account attribution (7-day click, 1-day view); campaign and ad totals match the account total for each month.
  • Google Ads — account 547-029-3556, campaigns by month without predicted value; totals reconciled with the account total. Auto-tagging is on; UTM tags are in the tracking template, the final URL suffix is empty. Conversions for the last few days may still be added.
  • Microsoft Ads — no account access; orders are counted via GA4 and Shopify tags.
  • Meta, Google and GA4 credit themselves with the same orders, so their revenue can't be summed or compared one-to-one with Shopify. The most honest shared metrics are MER and actual Shopify orders.
  • This is a one-off snapshot: data was exported from the accounts on 11/12/2026 and does not update automatically.
